Today was my very first day as an intern, and wow, it was definitely an eventful start!

Walking into a new IT digital company is exciting, but today taught me that things rarely go perfectly according to plan. Instead of letting first-day tech roadblocks slow me down, I had to think on my feet, adapt quickly, and find ways to keep moving forward.

## What I Actually Got Done Today

Even though I spent a good chunk of the day dealing with setup hurdles, I managed to cross off a ton of my day-one goals:

1. **Getting Plugged In:** I officially completed my company onboarding, got added to all the internal team communication channels, and started digging into the historical project documentation.
2. **The Personal Laptop Pivot:** My assigned company laptop didn't have its login password ready today. Instead of just sitting around, I pulled out my personal laptop, set up a temporary development environment, and used it to review files so I wouldn't fall behind.
3. **Playing IT Detective:** I spent a while trying to connect my phone to the office Wi-Fi for our mandatory mobile clock-in system. After running a bunch of network diagnostics, I isolated the issue to a hardware defect on my own phone rather than the office network.
4. **Keeping HR in the Loop:** Once I knew my phone was the problem, I immediately coordinated with HR to log my attendance manually. This kept everything professional and let me schedule a phone repair right after work without stressing.
5. **Downtime Coding:** I used my initial onboarding downtime to hop onto freeCodeCamp.org and brush up on my core JavaScript logic modules to prepare for upcoming tasks.

## Learning to Adapt on Day One

Today wasn't just about setting up a desk; it was a massive lesson in corporate problem-solving.

Working through today's hurdles showed me a few important things:

- **Resourcefulness is a superpower:** When company hardware isn't ready, using your own gear to stay productive keeps you on track and shows the team you're proactive.
- **Check your own tech first:** Before assuming the company network is down, double-check your own hardware. It saves a lot of troubleshooting time!
- **Over-communicate with HR:** Letting the right people know about an issue early prevents attendance misunderstandings and keeps things running smoothly.

> "Don't let a missing password stop you from making progress." Today proved that being flexible and maintaining a positive attitude is just as important as knowing how to code.

## What's Next on My Plate

I'm keeping this momentum going directly into tomorrow to get fully up to speed:

- Hitting up the company first thing in the morning to crack open my assigned company laptop.
- Reviewing previous sample code stored on the machine to understand how the team builds things.
- Keeping up with my morning logical reasoning and JavaScript exercises on freeCodeCamp.

It feels great to survive day one and have a dedicated space to document this journey. I can't wait to see what tomorrow brings!
